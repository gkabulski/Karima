02

What you’ll learn

Practical skills for every ride.

My coaching draws on the British Cycling technical syllabus, combining practical exercises, progressive challenges and individual feedback.

The skills covered depend on the coaching format, your experience and your objectives.

01

Bike handling & control

Develop control and confidence on your bike.

  • Balance and low-speed handling
  • Braking and emergency stopping
  • Cornering and body positioning
  • Gear selection and smooth pedalling
  • Looking behind while holding a steady line
  • Safe clipping in and unclipping
02

Group riding & positioning

Learn to ride confidently and predictably with others.

  • Following wheels and managing safe distances
  • Riding side by side and in single file
  • Observation and anticipation
  • Verbal and non-verbal communication
  • Moving through a group
  • Changing position and rotating at the front
  • Maintaining a consistent speed
03

Road skills & technique

Develop the skills to handle changing roads and conditions.

  • Climbing and descending
  • Gear choice and speed control
  • Body positioning and weight distribution
  • Cornering on different gradients and surfaces
  • Improving efficiency and coordination
04

Performance & progression

For riders ready to take their skills further.

  • Accelerating and sprinting
  • Starting alongside other riders
  • Responding to changes in pace
  • Positioning within a moving group
  • Combining skills in progressive riding scenarios

The aim isn’t simply to ride faster, but to develop the control, confidence and technique to ride more effectively.

Not every skill is covered in every session. Coaching content is selected according to the format, participants’ abilities and learning objectives.

03

What to expect

Structured coaching. Clear objectives. Real progression.

Every session is designed to help you develop practical cycling skills through focused drills, individual feedback and progressive challenges.

Whether you’re joining a women’s group session, booking 1:1 coaching or training with a private group, the approach is tailored to your ability, experience and goals.

  1. 01 / BEFORE YOUR SESSION

    Your starting point. Your goals.

    We’ll establish your current riding experience, confidence and the skills you’d like to develop.

    Each session has clear learning objectives, with activities selected to suit the participants and their abilities.

  2. 02 / DURING YOUR SESSION

    Learn. Practise. Progress.

    Sessions combine:

    • Technical demonstrations — Understand the key principles behind each skill.
    • Focused drills — Develop technique through practical, structured exercises.
    • Progressive challenges — Build skills step by step, increasing difficulty as your ability develops.
    • Individual feedback — Identify strengths, refine technique and work on areas for improvement.
    • Practical application — Put your skills into practice through realistic riding scenarios.

    Depending on the session, you may work individually, in pairs or as part of a group.

  3. 03 / AFTER YOUR SESSION

    Turn practice into progress.

    You’ll leave with a clearer understanding of your strengths, areas for development and the techniques you can continue practising independently.

    Progress might mean smoother cornering, more controlled braking, better positioning or greater confidence riding alongside others.

    The focus is on developing skills you can apply every time you ride.

04

Who it’s for

Coaching built around your level.

Coaching is primarily designed for beginner and intermediate road cyclists who want to improve their skills, confidence and technical ability.

You might be:

  • ✓Developing your cycling foundations
  • ✓Preparing for your first group ride
  • ✓Becoming more comfortable riding near others
  • ✓Returning to cycling
  • ✓Refining specific technical skills
  • ✓Progressing towards faster group rides or introductory racing

You don’t need to be fast or experienced. You just need to be willing to learn and practise.
